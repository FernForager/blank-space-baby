## What it is
**Triviela** (trivia + trivela, the outside-of-the-foot curling kick, Morg's coinage on Oct 15, 2026. One L. Portuguese spells the kick "trivela" and has no "triviela" variant, so the extra i is ours and deliberate, said trih-vee-EL-uh) is a public, standalone trivia game about the beautiful game: a daily puzzle in an arcade-cabinet look. It began as a word game for Morg's daughter's youth team (Seattle Celtic G14) and grew into its own thing on Oct 16, 2026. It's a standalone game first. Morg may still embed it in Carpool HQ's Arcade later, for fun. Built by Morg (owner, names everything, approves all player-facing text) with Claude. Domain: triviela.net, live since Oct 17, 2026: registered at Squarespace, with Cloudflare’s nameservers (anton and marjory) pointing it and www at the Pages project (DNSSEC off, the Squarespace parking records deleted, the registration itself to move to Cloudflare after the 60-day rule). The old address https://fernforager.github.io/blank-space-baby/ still works too, with its own saved progress. The repo, that address and the saved-progress keys (`bsb-…`) keep the old name Blank Space Baby on purpose.

**The lane (Oct 16, 2026):** trivia for the whole soccer family: the men's and the women's game, players and parents, US and global. Fun as trivia and fun as learning. Most daily games in the sport are guess-the-player puzzles for hardcore fans of men's European football. Triviela teaches (every card has a means line), covers rules, lingo, history and every league, and starts from the elementary basics: the offside rule, formations, field markings, ref signals, how many players, what a throw-in is, the positions. Soccer first: be stingy with non-soccer categories (Kid Slang is the model of a good one, its sentences all about soccer). **Design compass:** think Zach Gage. Simple rules, real stakes, a clean ending, a perfect day that's possible. No jokers, no second chances, no endless mode, no timer (a clock would punish reading the means lines, where the game teaches). Less is more: no instructions, labels or congrats text where icons, color and layout can carry it.

## The game as built (Oct 30, 2026: live on triviela.net, `main` f6313c9)
### The day
- **Nine cards a day, three green, three yellow, three red, and you play all nine** (Morg, Oct 27, 2026: he likes threes, a day of about 3 minutes, and a perfect day worth chasing). `PER = 3` in `build/build.py` is passed to the page (`__PER__`), so the build’s checks and the game agree. Green is easy (a 10-year-old player and a brand-new parent would both get it), yellow harder (a regular fan), red hardest (a real fan knows it, deep but never obscure). Green, yellow, red reads as easy to hard at a glance, like a traffic light (Morg, Oct 28, 2026), and each color keeps its own column, so color is never the only cue: for the common red-green color blindness, green stays as far from red as yellow does. Older notes and code comments may still say white for green: the deck’s `d` is 1, 2 or 3 either way.
- **The score is the count of right answers,** 7/9. All nine is **GOLAZO!** (Morg’s name and caps, Oct 19, 2026), the only celebration beyond a ball filling. A miss never ends the day. Streaks (Brace!, Hat Trick!) were tried and came out (Oct 26, 2026: hard to read). Next, Morg’s idea: a one-word title for every score from 0 to 9, from a whomp-whomp 0 up to GOLAZO!, with 2 Brace and 3 Hat Trick (hub to-do `scoretitles`, his words to write).
- **One board a day, the same for everyone,** seeded by puzzle number, reset at local midnight like Wordle. **Right now one fixed board** (`ONE_BOARD = True` in `build/build.py`, `ONE` in the page, Morg, Oct 28, 2026: until the game is tidy): every date plays #1, a finished day stays finished, and the end screen has no countdown. #1 is Morg’s own nine (hub decision `nine`, live Oct 29, 2026), pinned in `build/boards.json`, one card per category: Basics, Lingo and Technique green, Messi, EPL and MLS yellow, CR7, World Cup and NWSL red, each tile showing his category name. For the daily, set `ONE_BOARD` to False and bump the save version if #1’s cards changed.
- **Puzzle numbering:** `START` in `build/build.py` is the date of #1 (Oct 28, 2026 now, the first full nine-card day). Before it #1 plays early. During the playtest every number carries a β (`Triviela #1β`, Morg, Oct 26, 2026), set in the arcade face: at launch, drop the β from `share.title` and `image.score`, set `START` to launch day so #1 starts over, and bump the save version so testers’ old days don’t linger.
- **Repeats:** a card can come back, but never within 3 months (`REPEAT_DAYS = 91`), which needs about 297 cards a color. Until then cards come back as late as the deck allows, red (the smallest color) about every 89 days. `python3 build/build.py` prints the current numbers.

### The cabinet and the screen
- **The cabinet:** the phone is an arcade cabinet on HQ’s warm white, Celtic Green trim along the sides and under the black parts, a sign up top, a CRT screen in a black bezel (scanlines, glowing green text), and a black control panel with cream push-buttons. It is pinned: no rubber-band bounce, no double-tap zoom, keys press down under a finger (a touch listener, so Safari shows `:active`), and `.cab` pads by the safe-area insets for the Home Screen version.
- **The sign is a dot-matrix board** (`drawSign`, Morg, Oct 18, 2026: lean on the green grid, “pointillism”): a canvas of green dots at random brightness, the dots inside a 5×7 TRIVIELA (each font pixel 2×2 dots) glowing brighter, some letter dots dropping out and some stray dots flaring, seeded so every load looks the same, the dot pitch on whole device pixels, 84 px tall (70 on short phones). The name centers (`ox`): the menu’s three bars show only in the demo (How to play went on Oct 28, 2026, so the menu, a bottom sheet, holds only the demo’s Start over, behind a Keep playing / Start over pop-up set away from the finger). A tap outside any pop-up closes it. Under the whole sign, the subheading **“Daily ⚽ trivia shootout”** (Morg, Oct 26, 2026) in solid pale-green Press Start 2P capitals with no glow (a glow blurs pixel letters), 10.67 px (4 device pixels per font pixel at 3×), 8 px on short and narrow phones, one font pixel of tracking, a nudge that centers its ink. The ⚽ in any string is drawn by `th()` as the game’s own pixel ball (`SPR.sball`, 19×19, `tball()`, `.tball`: as tall as the capitals plus a pixel each way, its pixels on whole screen dots via `--bu`), so every player reads the sport in their own word, and screen readers skip it.
- **The slogan** “How well do you know the beautiful game?” sits in ink on the cabinet body between the screen and the cards, on the board only, one line at every size (15 px, 13 on short phones), centered on its letters (`text-box`).
- **Three balls on the screen** (Morg, Oct 26, 2026), one per card color over its column of tiles: each a true truncated icosahedron with a pentagon facing us, pressed onto a sphere and seen straight on (`BALLG`), so the seams curve and the patches shrink toward the rim, with a soft rim glow and a shine at the top left (`bglow1` to `bglow3`, `bshine`) so it reads as a hologram. Each ball, its dim dots, seams and glow, takes its card’s color (`.bi.k1` to `.k3`, `bdim1` to `bdim3`), the green one lit in the screen’s own green #5CF09A (`bgrn`). Nothing is lit at the start, and a part not earned stays dim in the ball’s color, never black. **The balls fill from the rim in** (`stage()`, Morg, Oct 28, 2026: least exciting to best): a color’s first right answer lights its whole outside (panels 5 to 14, the pentagons and the hexagons at the edge), the second the five hexagons round the center (0 to 4), the third the center pentagon alone (15), whichever cards they were. **A ball filling** (Morg: a cherry on top, GOLAZO! stays the peak): it spins once (`.spin`, the rim and panels only, so the light stays put), pops (`.ball.fill`), flashes, and a ring of twelve dots bursts from its rim in its color and fades (`sparks()`). **A perfect day:** the last ball’s moment (0.9 s), then all three glow white and bob, the slivers past the rim light, then the dot-matrix goal scene flickers on (`SCENES`, drawn as text in `tools/scene.py`: the goal and its net, the striker with arms up, the shot’s dotted flight) with the shout GOLAZO! over it (`.shout`, arcade face), clearing after about 2.5 s. Reduce Motion drops the spin, the pop, the sparks and the wave.
- **The screen hugs its balls** (Morg, Oct 28, 2026): on the board the screen’s natural height is the balls plus `--hug` (30 px) above and below, worked out from the width (the balls are .3 of the screen’s inner width), and room to spare goes two thirds to the cards and a third to the screen, so the tiles grow taller (about 95 px on Morg’s phone).
- **Below the cards, all day** (`#p-hint`, the board only, gone once the day is over, Morg, Oct 28, 2026, his layout): two stacked lines on the panel under the tiles, “Pick a card” in the screen’s glowing green arcade capitals (`.pick`, slot `pick`), and under it “easier”, the three card colors (`rcard`), “harder” (`.legend`, spoken as `board.legend`).
- **Attract mode:** until the day’s first tap (`S.tapped`, saved, so a tap that went Back still counts), a wave of green light runs corner to corner across the tiles every 3.2 s (`#p-board.attract`, `chase`), like an arcade cabinet’s chase lights. Made after Morg’s kids froze on the first screen (Oct 19, 2026).

### The board and its tiles
- **Nine cream keys, a color per column** (green, yellow, red), three rows. Tiles 0 to 2 are green, 3 to 5 yellow, 6 to 8 red (the deal’s order), and `spot(i)` is a card’s place in its column, 0 to 2, the same spot in the play screen’s grid, the end grid, the share and the picture, so the same spot is the same card for everyone.
- **Every tile is a text box** (Morg, Oct 28, 2026: best in class, like a game’s, so text swaps easily and can be translated): `face()` sets its category on the `tag` row (one line, tightened, never cut: the deck’s tag from `TAG`, or the card’s own `tag`, a string `tag.*`) and its hook on the `hook` row (the word or name from its sentence that is never the answer: two lines at most at 19, 18 or 16 px by phone, shrinking to fit but never below the row’s `shrink` floor, 70%). `orph()` keeps the last two words of any text of three words or more together when the pair is 16 letters or fewer (hooks, sentences, meaning lines), `keep()` keeps hyphenated words and ranges whole, and no word ever breaks. A referee’s card in the tile’s top-right corner shows its color (10×14, `.tile.c1` to `.c3`). `tools/tilecheck.py` holds every card in the draw to all of this at three sizes and exits 1 if a card that plays breaks a rule (14 hooks are still one word too wide for the smallest phone, none on the fixed board: the editor shortens them before the daily comes back). Category names must stay short in any translation.
- **A played tile** is pressed a shade darker (`.tile.done`, `.tile.miss`) and shows the top ball in black and white (`bwBall()`: `BALLG`’s own drawing, white hexagons, ink pentagons and seams, a soft shade, a thin ink edge, 34 px, 28 on short phones) or a smooth red ✕ in the same ink outline (`bwMiss()`). Morg, Oct 28, 2026: “why not just use our ball from the top but color it black and white and scale down?” The rule: the pixel ball lives only in the sign, and everywhere a result shows it is the smooth ball or ✕.
- **Every tile opens on one tap,** and a reload reopens the open card. A card is committed only once answered: **Back** (Morg, Oct 26, 2026: you play all nine anyway and the score is the count, so peeking changes nothing) returns it to the board unanswered, its three choices kept in place for next time. The saved `path` is the order answered, not opened.

### A card
- One sentence with a blinking dashed blank (26 px, 23 over 52 characters, 21 over 70, balanced) and three cream keys: the answer and two wrong ones. Until answered, the Back key sits quiet at the left of the go row, so it never reads as a fourth answer. Right = green with a pixel ✓, and the blank fills in glowing green. Wrong = red with a pixel ✕ and a shake, the other choice dims, and the blank fills white with a dashed underline. **Meanings show only on the buttons** (the right answer’s, plus your own wrong pick’s), two lines at most, balanced, and the untouched choice stays unexplained. Screen readers hear a meaning line only once it shows (the audit).
- **Sentence rendering, in order:** escape → `orph()` → swap the answer for a blank glued to its punctuation, so they never split → `keep()` → `num()` (digits, GOLAZO! and parentheses in the arcade face). Punctuation never wraps away from the word or blank it touches.
- **The right answer never lands in the same slot three times running** (counting answered cards). The two wrong answers and the button order are seeded, so everyone sees the same three (“The draw and the choices” below).
- **After an answer:** a full-width Next key at the panel’s bottom (its space held, so no key moves, layout A: the panel is the controller), the beta’s Flag and Lock either side of it on preview links, and the whole screen still taps through. **The tap shield** (`guard()`): a touch within 350 ms of a new screen or a pop-up opening or closing is dropped, since a quick double tap on a tile used to answer the card unread.
- **The grid** in the top right of the play screen: the 3×3, every card in its spot, a lit dot for right, a pixel ✕ for a miss, a dim ring still to play, and the open card blinking in its spot.
- A phone turned sideways, or a very short window, scrolls to the question instead of hiding it (the audit). A proper sideways layout is for later.

### The draw and the choices
- **The draw** is seeded by the puzzle number, so every phone deals the same board (`dealDay`). Each color keeps a line of its cards, oldest first. A day deals from a shuffle of the 24 oldest in each line (`WINDOW`), and dealt cards go to the back, so a card comes back as late as the deck allows. The page replays every day from #1 to rebuild the lines (about 30 ms for a year), and before #1 it quietly deals `PREROLL` days (40) that nobody plays, so the cards Morg met on the early test boards sit at the back. Raise `PREROLL` for another fresh run, and decide at launch whether to keep it.
- **Guardrails** (`fits`, `want`, checked by `tools/drawcheck.py` over a year): at least one women’s game card and one Soccer Rules card, at most 2 cards from one league (`LEAGUE_MAX` in `build/build.py`, both EPL decks count as EPL, Club Nicknames go by their source site, the Champions League counts as one league, Basics is capped like a league), at most one slang card a day, Kid Slang or Parent Slang, and never a red one (Morg, Oct 16, 2026), and no two cards that give each other away (one’s answer in the other’s sentence, hook or means line, or a shared answer or hook, with a spelled number from two to twenty counting as its digits).
- **A pinned day** (`PIN`, from `build/boards.json`: puzzle number to nine ids in board order, three green, three yellow, three red) deals Morg’s cards in his order, skips the guardrails, and sends them to the back of their lines like any dealt card. `pinned()` in `build/build.py` checks every id is in the draw, in its spot’s color, on one board only and never a red slang card, and the build stops otherwise. Unpinned days deal as before. A pinned board must be live by the evening before its day (Seattle time): `tools/golive.py` stops on one that changes a day already under way.
- **The choices** (`choices()`) are seeded per day and card, so everyone sees the same three in the same order, and they hold still (the audit, Oct 30, 2026): every candidate is ranked by its own hash, and near misses and the button order have their own random streams, so a card in or out of a deck never moves another card’s choices. Morg’s own two wrong answers (`wr`) win when a card has them. **A number answer gets two made-up near misses** (`near2`, Morg, Oct 19, 2026: borrowed numbers were wildly off): a count up to 12 moves by 1 to 3, a bigger one by 10 to 35 percent at the answer’s own roundness, a year by 1 to 10 (by 4 or 8 for a World Cup, Euro or Olympics), never past the puzzle’s own year unless the answer is, a future year stays future, a spelled count (four, eight) gets spelled near misses, each lands above or below at random so the answer isn’t always the middle one, and a number already in the sentence or on the tile is never used. A number right after a hedge (more than, about, nearly, up to) keeps the borrowed numbers. So a number card must be exact or hedge: a near miss can’t be a second right answer when the fact is exact. **Otherwise the two wrong answers come from the card’s own kind** (`k`) in its deck: the best same-kind pair first, then any kind, never one that could fill the same blank (`groups`, `clash`), never one already in the sentence or on the tile (the card’s own answer left out of that test, so a card about the assistant referee can offer referee), and a plural answer never draws 1.
- **The fingerprint** of a card (its answer, sentence, means, hook, color, kind and the wrong answers it can draw, `fingerprint()` in the build) is what every verdict is tied to, so any change sends the card back to all three reviewers. Morg’s flags and locks hold to the card’s own fields alone (`mark_hash`: the same fields and his own wrong answers, never its deck-mates’), so a change elsewhere in the deck never lapses a flag or breaks a lock (the audit’s open item, fixed Oct 30, 2026). A card with `wr` needs no kind, and adding `wr` never sends its deck-mates back (the audit). Losing a wrong answer never sends a card back, since fewer decoys can’t add a second right answer.

### Saved on the phone, results and midnight
- **`bsb-daily`** (`v: 6`) holds today’s board: the day, a random token for the board (`tk`), the nine cards themselves with their choices as dealt (so a deck change mid-day can’t alter a started board), each tile’s result, the open card, the order answered (`path`), the last two answer slots, `tapped`, `sent` and `sending`. It saves on every tap. A finished day reopens to its end screen. In the daily, a finished or untouched day rolls over at local midnight (`dayOf`, `today()`), a day in progress stays until it’s finished or the page reloads, and a day finished after its own midnight (`late`) keeps its end screen, the countdown giving way to the demo’s Play #N key (`#e-next`, `endRow()`), one tap to the new board (Morg, Oct 30, 2026, hub decision `lateday`: the alternative, flipping to the new board as midnight does, would take a just-finished board away under the thumb). A save from another version or day starts fresh (bump `v` whenever a change would make an old save wrong). **Two tabs on one day** merge their answers on every save, every other tab’s save and every return to the tab (`merge`, `catchUp`), so a stale tab never wipes a finished day (the audit).
- **Anonymous results** (Morg, Oct 19, 2026): a finished board posts once to `/api/result` (the puzzle, day 0 is #1, its nine card ids, each one’s result, the tap order and the board’s token, so a lost reply can never count a board twice), `S.sent`, and tries again on the next visit if there was no signal, one post at a time shared between tabs (`S.sending`). Nothing about who played: no name, no device id, no IP. A day finished with no signal and then replaced by a new day waits in `bsb-unsent` (never on an address without the service), and every visit sends the oldest one waiting (`resend()`), dropping one the server refuses for good. Rows go to the `results` table with host `real` (triviela.net) or `beta` (preview links). To read them: query `SELECT day, score, ids, res, path, host, at FROM results ORDER BY id` with the Cloudflare tool, save the reply, run `python3 tools/results.py FILE` (players only, or `--beta` to count Morg’s testing): boards and average score per puzzle, each color’s hit rate, which color players tap first, and the cards missed most.

### Text, fonts and sprites
- **The text system** (Morg, Oct 26, 2026: `research/text-system.md`): every word the game shows or says, apart from the cards, lives in `build/text/en.json` (Google’s ARB shape: an id, its English, and `@id` with a note on where it shows, its slot and its placeholders like `{n}`). The game never holds English itself: the markup names a string with `data-t` (and `data-t-label`, `data-t-alt`), the script uses `tx(id, vars)` for plain text and `th(id, vars)` for typeset HTML (digits, #1 and GOLAZO! in the arcade face, punctuation glued, the ⚽ drawn), and text it writes later carries `data-tid`. `build/text/slots.json` is the type table: every place text appears, with its face, size per phone, line height, tracking, alignment, wrapping (one line, balanced, pretty), line limit, `trim` (centered on its letters), `nudge`, `fit` (how far a too-wide word may tighten its letters, in device pixels), `shrink` (a hook’s floor) and `fallback` (a size for a browser without container units). The build turns it into the game’s text CSS, so text never gets a size or face anywhere else in the stylesheet. `build/strings.py` checks both (notes, placeholders, house style, every id the game asks for in markup, `th()` and `data-tid`, and no “soccer” or “football” in any string) and the build stops on a problem. A new string: add it to en.json with a note and a slot, use it with `tx`/`th` or `data-t`, run the build and `tools/textcheck.py`. Morg approves every string.
- **Morg’s text editor:** on a preview link, `?text` (e.g. https://daily.blank-space-baby.pages.dev/?text) adds a bar: Tap to edit, then tap any text on the screen to change it, and the real screen redraws as he types, with a fit check against its slot, the house style and its blanks. All text lists every string, the spoken ones too. His changes wait on the phone until Send posts them to `/api/text` (`functions/api/text.js`), into the `texts` table. The hub’s “Apply my text edits” ask: query `SELECT id, body, at FROM texts WHERE applied = 0 ORDER BY id` with the Cloudflare tool, save the reply, run `python3 tools/textedits.py FILE` (it applies only the newest batch, skips any string that changed since his edit, and holds an edit to the game’s name or address or one adding a web address for his OK), rebuild, run `tools/textcheck.py`, push to `daily`, run the `UPDATE texts SET applied = 1 …` it prints, and reply with what changed.
- **Fonts:** Press Start 2P (the sign’s subheading, the Pick a card line, digits, GOLAZO!, parentheses, the score, the Next key, the countdown) and Pixelify Sans (everything else, the body at weight 600, meaning lines at 500), both loaded from Google Fonts by this build (`build/template.html`). Pixelify draws 5 like an S, Z like a 2, G like a 6 and ( ) like curly braces, and its fi ligature looks like an A, so ligatures are off and every digit, GOLAZO! and parentheses are set in Press Start 2P (`.num`, `ARCADE`, with `.num.po` and `.num.pc` keeping the parentheses’ gaps). Digits are 8/11 of the text around them (as tall as Pixelify’s capitals) and drop .125em, since Press Start 2P draws an empty pixel row under every glyph. Every arcade-face slot says weight 400, since the face has no bold and a browser would smear one on. Keep all of these fixes, and check any new font for the same problems. Press Start 2P is 8 font pixels to the em, so it is sharp only at a whole number of device pixels per font pixel (8 px, 10.67 px at 3×, 24 px): prefer those sizes for any new arcade-face slot. The cleaned self-hosted fonts in `docs/fonts/` (Triviela Pixel and Triviela Arcade, sharp text stage 2) and their tooling in `build/font/` belong to the branch `beta-oct22` and are not loaded by this build.
- **Sprites:** `SPR` holds pixel art as rows of characters, drawn by `svg(name, scale, cls, color)`. A pixel sprite must land on whole device pixels (the phone is 3×, some are 2×), and the sign’s ball sizes itself by `--bu` for that. Pentagons and the balls are vector shapes (`pent()`, `BALLG`), never pixel sprites: a pixel grid can’t hold their 36° and 72° edges. The rule for marks: the pixel ✕ goes with the pixel ball, the smooth ✕ with the smooth ball.
- **Accessibility:** every tile and key has a spoken label from the strings (`tile.label`, `tile.right`, `tile.missed`, the colors, `end.row`: “Green: 2 of 3”), the legend and the ⚽ are skipped or described, the countdown is not live, and meanings are hidden until shown. The blank reads as “blank” (`role="img"`), focus follows the screen (the card when one opens, the board on the way back, the end screen), and Review says “3 of 9, right” or “3 of 9, missed” in the tiles’ own words.

### House style for all text (strict)
- **Never soccer or football (Morg, Oct 26, 2026):** to keep the game global, nothing the game shows, says or sends uses either word: the sign, labels, the share and its picture, the link preview, the Home Screen name, spoken labels. Icons, emoji and art carry the sport, and “the beautiful game” names it (the slogan exists for exactly this reason). Cards try to avoid both words too, keeping one only where the word is the fact (soccer mom, Soccer City, total football, footy). Internal names (deck ids, code, these notes) don’t reach players and can stay. 29 cards still say one in passing: the editor rewords them.
- One plain sentence per card, deadpan school-workbook tone. The answer supplies the fun.
- No period at the end (!, ?, ?! and … are fine). **Never semicolons, anywhere.** Use commas, colons, em dashes (closed up: word—word) or periods.
- Curly quotes and apostrophes only. Scores and ranges use an en dash (6–0, 2025–26). American spelling, Merriam-Webster’s (kickoff, halftime, shin guard, Euro not EURO).
- Punctuation never wraps away from the word or blank it touches. Hyphenated words and ranges never break across lines (the code handles both).
- Slang and lingo decks (Kid Slang, Parent Slang, Soccer Lingo: `alpha`, `parents`, `soccer`, `lingo-more`): every sentence is about soccer. No proper names, real or made up. Use roles (the coach, the ref, the keeper, the striker, the captain) and Mom, Dad, Grandpa, Grandma.
- The game’s two words for its 3×3 are grid and board (Morg, Oct 28, 2026): the word scoreboard is gone from every label.
- Trivia decks: plain factual sentences, real names allowed. Clubs and national teams take British plural verbs (“Arsenal are”, “Aston Villa have”), Morg's call on Oct 16, 2026.
- Kid-safe: anything borderline (sexual, drugs, alcohol, slurs, mean-spirited, private-life gossip) is out. When unsure, cut it.
- No song lyrics or quoted lines from songs, books or films, not even fragments.
