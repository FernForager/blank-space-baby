# Card notes from the hook and difficulty pass (Oct 16, 2026)

The agents that wrote and checked every card’s hook and difficulty also flagged 39 cards for Morg’s review. Nothing here was changed: no wording, no facts. Card numbers count from 1, in deck order (the review page’s order for the soccer decks).

## Patterns across decks

These come from the twelve agents’ remarks. Each one affects many cards, so fixing the pattern beats fixing cards one by one.

1. **Two right answers in the slang decks.** Groups keep synonyms apart but not opposites, so a praise card (fire, lit, gas, tuff, peak, bussin) can draw mid or cringe as a wrong answer that also fits, and the other way round. Fresh and def can draw wack. Grouping mid and cringe with the praise words fixes most of Kid Slang. Parent Slang may need reworded sentences instead.
2. **Men’s and women’s answers mixed in one kind.** In Soccer Legends (player) and Awards (keeper, coach), a women’s card usually shows the only woman’s name among the three buttons, so it plays as easy by elimination. Splitting those kinds by men’s and women’s game turns them into real questions.
3. **Numbers that give themselves away or coin-flip.** Some number kinds hold absurd wrong answers (974 minutes, a stadium that holds 27 fans), which makes cards easier than their facts. Others put close numbers side by side (La Liga’s founding years 1898, 1899, 1902 and 1903, Serie A’s four year cards), which makes them a guess.
4. **Units in Soccer Rules.** The measure kind mixes yards, feet and inches, so the unit can give the answer away (5 inches is the only inches answer). Splitting it by unit fixes this.
5. **“an” before the blank.** Soccer Rules 33 (“punished with an”), Soccer Rules 45 (“judging an”) and EPL (more) 42 (“an Etihad Stadium stand”) fit only the right answer.
6. **Club Nicknames that answer themselves.** Six sentences explain their own answer: munitions for Gunners, trees for Cherries, birds for parakeets, “a bright orange-red” for vermilion, farming for cucumber growers and the sound of a canary. They play as honest white tiles, but they test reading more than soccer.
7. **The same fact in more than one deck.** Club nicknames repeat across EPL, EPL (more) and Club Nicknames, and Pelé’s three World Cups, Messi’s two Golden Balls, Kaká in 2007, Argentina–France in 2022 and others repeat across Legends, Awards and the international decks. The draw already keeps two versions of one fact off the same board, but cutting the copies would free up variety.
8. **Year hooks.** Many Awards and International Women’s sentences name nothing but the answer, so their tile can only show a year (2014, 2025). Better tiles would need sentence edits, not hook edits.
9. **Whites are scarce in the league decks.** Serie A and Ligue 1 have no white cards and La Liga, Bundesliga and both EPL decks have 1 or 2 each. Most whites now come from Soccer Lingo, Soccer Rules and the two slang decks. A basics batch of white cards is the fix.
10. **One slang sentence isn’t about soccer.** Kid Slang card 78 (cottagecore, Grandma’s picnic).
11. **Spelling.** Awards card 55 spells Emiliano Martinez without the accent (card 25 has Martínez). Two Awards means lines spell Euro as EURO.
12. **Facts newer than the agents know** (not checked, since the pass was hooks and tags only): the 2026 World Cup results, the 2025 award winners, Le Volcan as the Vélodrome’s nickname (Ligue 1 card 29), and three Taylor Swift facts (the Toy Story 5 song at No. 1, the Songwriters Hall of Fame at 36, 10 weeks at No. 1 for The Fate of Ophelia).
13. **Current facts to recheck each season:** Inter as reigning champions, Fàbregas at Como, Kompany’s titles, Zidane as France coach, Ligue 1’s 34 matchdays, the 2026–27 matchday count and the newly promoted Levante and Paris FC.
14. **The 12-character hook limit** shuts out Saint-Étienne, Bayern Munich, Athletic Club and Werder Bremen (13 each), so those cards fell back on words like “nickname” or “home games.” A 13-character limit would let them through, at a small cost on the smallest phones.
