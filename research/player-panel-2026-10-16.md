# Triviela panel roadmap (18 players, merged)

## 1. The headline

Almost everyone loved the game as described: the same 18 cards for everyone, picking your own tile, one miss ends the day, and the line that teaches you something. What would make most of them quit has nothing to do with features. It's going out on card 2 of the first ball to something they could never know, or catching one wrong card. They asked you to build very little, make ball one fair, make every card true, and let the group chat do the rest.

## 2. What nearly everyone agrees on

- **Same cards for everyone that day, with a day number:** 18 of 18. Every one of them says the group chat is the reason to come back. Several said, in close to the same words, "the group chat is the leaderboard."
- **No accounts, leaderboards, jokers, extra lives or hints:** 18 of 18.
- **Showing the right answer and its meaning when you miss:** 18 of 18 called it the consolation prize. A loss turns into a fact you repeat at practice or dinner. The game already fills in the blank, so this mostly exists.
- **A one-tap, plain-text share that shows how far you got** (a ⚫ per right answer, a 🟥 where you went out, the day number, a short link): 17 of 18. The 18th, June (10), wants the dots but not the red square. Nobody wanted an image, hashtags or "Can you beat me?" text.
- **No endless or practice mode:** 17 of 18. June didn't mention it.
- **The anonymous right/wrong counter, after launch:** 18 of 18. It's the only feature everyone asked for.
- **Chalkboard picture cards, after launch, as a few cards in the daily mix (not a new mode):** 18 of 18.
- **Going out early on ball one as the top reason to quit:** 13 of 18. Every kid, parent, rec player, lifelong fan and new fan said it, and so did Noor and Tessa.
- **Order the balls easy, then medium, then hard:** 14 asked for it outright, and nobody objected.
- **No push notifications or streak guilt:** 15 named it. Nobody asked for notifications.
- **No timer:** 9 named it. Nobody asked for one.
- **No category picker for the daily:** 10 named it. They want the odd mix to stay.
- **No separate Basics mode:** nobody wanted one. The fix is an easy tier that really is easy.
- **Test with real players before going public:** 11 of 18.
- **One wrong or stale card loses them for good:** 10 of 18. Those 10 are mostly high schoolers, college players, pros and lifelong fans.

## 3. Where groups split, and what it means

- **How easy is easy?** Kids, parents, rec players, lifelong fans and new fans want ball one easy for them. The high schoolers, college players and pros say six baby cards feel like "a toll booth" (Tommy). Josie warned that a boring easy ball invites a careless miss. Going easy, then medium, then hard settles most of it. Ball one goes fast for experts and ball three stays truly hard. "Easy" also has to mean the overlap: things a 10-year-old, a sideline mom and a 58-year-old all know (famous players, basic rules, the World Cup). It can't mean easy for a 10-year-old only (June) or easy for a dad who watches the Bundesliga (Jen). That's my reading of what they said together.
- **What hard means:** Tommy, Tess, Reid, Mateo and Theo L all said hard should mean knowing the game (Laws detail, tactics, real history). It should not mean obscure dates, stadiums or nicknames. Several named Liga MX and Bundesliga club nicknames as the cards that felt like a coin flip.
- **Kid Slang:** The kids and parents love it. It's where kids beat Dad and moms beat dads. Teens and twenty-somethings find it cringe but funny once. The lifelong fans got knocked out on ball one by it and felt insulted. Twelve of 18 want it limited. Reid, Jess and Gary asked for at most one a day. Mateo, Noor and Tessa want real kids to check every slang card. Their warning: "one cringe card gets screenshotted and the game becomes a joke."
- **The women's game:** Noor, Tessa, Josie and Tess (all women players) and Reid want it in every daily, not walled off in its own category. Tessa and Josie flagged that a card like "Arsenal won the 2025–26 title" must say men's or women's. Your own example card is one of them. Tess suggested a rule like at least 6 of the 18 cards being about the women's game or neither (rules, lingo). The parents and lifelong fans didn't raise it either way.
- **This season or history:** Peggy and Gary want a legends or history card every day and fewer this-season facts. The pros want this-season facts dated and checked, or cut. The two fit together: fewer current cards, and every one of them dated.
- **Streaks:** Noor, Theo L and Danny want one at launch, and 12 more want a quiet one later. June, Theo N and Peggy are against it. Peggy said "a streak turns a pleasure into a chore." Everyone who wants one said: days played, no flames, no nagging. So it can wait, and if it comes, it stays quiet.
- **The goal moment:** Theo N (10) wants it loud and big. Mateo, Theo L, Tommy, Tess, Reid, Jess and Dave want no slow animations. Eight adults said no sound by default (car lines, trains, sleeping kids). That points to a quick visual punch under a second, with no sound. That's my judgment.
- **Dark mode:** 12 would like it later because they play in bed. Mateo, Jen, Peggy and Gary said don't bother, and Peggy and Gary said fix the text size first. Nobody called it a launch need.
- **Tile choice is felt, not real (worth knowing):** Josie and Jess pointed out that you must answer all six tiles anyway, so the order never changes your result. Most of the panel still calls picking tiles their favorite part and their "strategy." It does change one thing: how many ⚫ show in your share before the 🟥. Keep it as feel, and don't build systems on top of it as if it mattered.

## 4. The roadmap

**BEFORE LAUNCH**

1. **Build the daily game.** Same 18 cards for everyone by date, numbered days, ball one easy, ball two medium, ball three hard. One miss ends it, goals are banked, and the game remembers you already played and shows your result if you reopen it. It saves after every answer so a dead tab or a tunnel never costs the day. Add a few draw rules too: at most one Kid Slang card a day, no two cards from the same league on one ball, a women's game share every day, and at least one history card.
   - Why: this is the whole game, and the draw rules decide who it feels made for. Saving after every answer came from Jen, Greg, Jess and Dave.
   - Serves: everyone.
2. **Do a content pass on the cards you have (editing, no code).** Write a hook word for every card in the daily pool. It should be readable out loud, say what kind of card it is, and never give away the answer. Date-stamp and re-check every this-season fact. Label men's or women's wherever it could be either. Check that each card has one clearly right answer and two believable wrong ones. Check Laws cards word for word against IFAB. Count how many days of cards you have: it's the smallest tier divided by 6.
   - Why: hook words make the tile choice work, and trust matters most when one miss ends your day. This is editor's work, which suits you.
   - Serves: pros, college players, high schoolers, lifelong fans, new fans (hook words) and kids (readable hooks).
3. **Build the end screen and share text.** Show the card that got you with its answer and meaning line, your result, a Share button and a countdown to tomorrow. The share is plain text: day number, result word, ⚫ rows with the 🟥, short link. Test it by pasting into iMessage and WhatsApp on a real phone.
   - Why: the share is the only way it spreads, and the countdown brings people back.
   - Serves: everyone. WhatsApp matters for rec players, college players and family abroad.
4. **Check it on a phone.** Make sentence text bigger and plainer for older eyes. Space the answer buttons and add a short pause so a stray tap during a transition doesn't count. Keep the ✓ and ✕ for colorblind players. Keep the goal moment quick.
   - Why: Peggy and Gary will quit over squinting, and Reid nearly quit over a fat-finger Nil.
   - Serves: lifelong fans, kids, parents, pros, college players.
5. **Soft launch for two weeks.** Start with the G14 team and its parents, plus a rec team chat and a high school chat. Have your daughter and two teammates check every Kid Slang and easy card first. Watch which cards cause ball-one Nils and move them up a tier.
   - Why: "Easy as judged by a careful editor is not easy for me on the train" (Jess). Real misses tell you fast and cost nothing.
   - Serves: everyone, especially kids, parents and new fans.

**AFTER LAUNCH**

6. **Add the anonymous right/wrong counter.** Re-sort difficulty monthly from real results.
   - Why: it fixes mis-tagged easy cards without you guessing, and it keeps a Hat trick rare but possible.
   - Serves: all 18 asked for it.
7. **Small extras, only if players ask:**
   - a quiet days-played number and your last 7 results (15 want some version)
   - a read-only look at the rest of the day's cards after you're out (8 asked: June, Theo N, Josie, Tommy, Tess, Greg, Dani, Danny)
   - a tiny "this card is wrong?" email link

   Only Tess and Reid asked for the email link. I'd still lean toward it because it costs almost nothing and protects accuracy (my judgment).
   - Serves: high schoolers, new fans, kids, pros.
8. **Picture cards, one kind, 10 to 20 cards, mixed into the daily.** Offside is the most requested. Reid argues for ref signals and field markings first because they leave no room to argue, and says offside should wait until the drawing is clear enough that nobody can dispute it.
   - Why: it's the one new thing nothing else offers, and it lets players beat parents. Prove one kind before drawing more.
   - Serves: all 18, especially players, sideline parents and new fans.

Then stop and watch. Add cards, not features. Dark mode only if lots of people ask.

## 5. The cut list

- **Accounts, profiles, leaderboards, friend lists:** all 18.
- **Jokers, hints, extra lives, "watch an ad to continue":** all 18. Theo L: "The sting is the product."
- **Endless mode, practice mode, replaying today:** 17. Danny: "The second I can play unlimited, the daily one stops mattering."
- **Push notifications and streak nags:** 15. Greg: "My daughter texting me her score is the notification."
- **Category picker or favorite leagues:** 10, including Greg, Tessa, Josie, Reid, Dave and Peggy. It splits the shared day.
- **Timers or speed scoring:** 9, led by the kids, Jen and the bus and train riders.
- **An archive of past days at launch:** 9, including Mateo, Greg, Reid, Josie and Dave. Noor and Theo L would like one someday.
- **A separate Basics mode:** nobody wanted it on the menu. Put basic cards in the easy tier. Reid and Tommy want basic cards kept out of the shared 18 altogether.
- **Badges, XP, avatars, unlockables, confetti, "Great job!", sound on by default:** most of the panel, kids included. Theo N: "That's the stuff that makes Mom take the iPad away."
- **More categories before the current ones are balanced and accurate:** Tessa, Theo L, Tommy, Tess, Peggy, Dani, Josie. Tess: "1,400 accurate cards beat 3,000 shaky ones."
- **Share images and pre-written brag text:** Mateo, Gary, Tessa, Theo L.
- **An app store app:** Noor, Peggy, Gary, Danny. A Home Screen link is enough.
- **A separate kids' version:** June. She wants the same cards as Dad.
- **A "pro" difficulty mode:** Reid. Make the hard cards hard instead.
- **Player photos:** Tess. Legal trouble, and it turns into a face quiz.
- **EA FC-style ratings:** Tessa, Theo L.

## 6. Open decisions for you

1. **Locking the middle tile until last.** 11 like it as the shot on goal, led by Tess, Reid, Gary and Tessa. Jess and Dave want it only if the middle tile holds the hardest card of the six, and Tommy wants it hardest too. Josie and June call it unneeded, and Danny says playtest it. My lean: keep the lock and make the middle the hardest card, so the "shot" is real.
2. **Should players know ball one is the easy ball?** Danny wants to know. Your no-labels rule says let the game show it. A quiet visual change from ball to ball could do it without words.
3. **The share grid.** Red square or not: 17 want it, June doesn't. Name the category you went out on ("out on Liga MX")? Reid and Jess want it. Dave, Theo L, Danny, Tess and Mateo call that a spoiler. My lean: keep the 🟥 and leave the category out.
4. **Reset time.** Seven named local midnight, Jen wants midnight Pacific, and Josie and Tommy want one time for everyone so teammates abroad match. My lean: local midnight with the same number for each date, the way Wordle does it. Everyone who plays #42 gets the same cards.
5. **Where Kid Slang goes.** Tess wants it in the easy ball only. Peggy and Gary want it kept out of ball one. My lean: one a day, in ball two, so it can never block anyone's first goal.
6. **The reading font.** Peggy and Gary want sentences plainer than pixel text. The look is the brand. You decide how far it bends.
7. **Repeats.** Tommy wants no repeats and about 90 days of cards banked before launch. Dave says recycle, nobody remembers a card from three months ago. Theo L thought 1,400 cards was more than a year, but at 18 a day it's about 11 weeks, and less if the hard pile is the smallest (my math).
8. **Showing results to players.** Should the counter stay invisible or show "X% got this card" after you finish? Noor, Tessa, Theo L and Mateo want the number shown. Most didn't say.
9. **How much women's game per day.** Tess proposed at least 6 of 18. You set the number.

## 7. Three quotes

- "A hard card in my first six feels like getting sent off in the warm-up." (Dave, 35, rec player)
- "Soccer people screenshot errors faster than they share scores." (Tess, 29, retired pro)
- "As long as I can read it without squinting and the first ball doesn't knock me out over a word my granddaughter invented last Tuesday." (Peggy, 58, lifelong fan)

Where I wrote "my lean," "my judgment" or "my math," that's my view, not the panel's.
