# Triviela panel, round two: one roadmap

## 1. The headline

The panel loved the daily as you've locked it, in every age group. They loved picking your own tiles, the card colors, one miss ending the day and the means line. The worries are almost all about trust and fairness, not missing features. White tiles have to be easy for a beginner, red tiles have to be deep but gettable, every fact has to be right and you have to be able to read the board. The one real design argument is whether showing the colors turns the "gamble" into an order that's solved by day two.

## 2. What nearly everyone agrees on

- **One miss ends the day, and goals stay banked.** 18 of 18 want to keep it. Nobody asked for a second chance.
- **The means line is the heart of it.** 18 of 18 named it, and most want it after every answer, especially the miss.
- **The group chat is the leaderboard.** 18 of 18 said no accounts and no leaderboards.
- **The card colors read instantly.** 16 of 18 understood white, yellow and red with no explanation.
- **Readability is the biggest build risk.** 17 of 18 raised text size, hook length or tap size. That means pixel text on 18 small tiles, read in a car, on a train, with readers on or by a slow 10-year-old reader.
- **No 6 × 6 share grid.** No one wants it. Most call it clutter in a group text.
- **Offside picture cards are the best later idea.** 18 of 18 listed them, and all 18 said after launch.
- **An anonymous difficulty counter.** 18 of 18 want it, invisible and after launch.
- **No timer** (14 of 18). **No streak guilt or push notifications** (all who mentioned it). **No more non-soccer categories** (13 of 18).
- **Hard should mean real knowledge.** About 13 of 18 said hard should be Laws nuance, tactics, women's depth and history across eras. It should not be dates, transfer fees, founding years or this season's managers.
- **One wrong or stale fact gets screenshotted and kills trust.** 11 of 18 said so, the pros most strongly.
- **Too much men's European club content would lose them.** About 12 of 18 said so. Liga MX and Bundesliga cards in the white tier came up again and again.
- **The day has to survive real life.** About 10 of 18 named this. A run should survive a locked phone, a tunnel or an app switch. A finished day should reopen to your result, and you can't replay it.
- **Test with real people before launch.** About 12 of 18 said one real team chat for a week beats any panel.

## 3. Where groups split, and what it means

**Show the colors or hide them?**
- **For hiding (2):** Ren (college) and Mateo (college). Every chip counts the same, so whites, then yellows, then reds is always the right order, and the choice is fake by day two. Mateo's fix: hide the color and flip it after you answer.
- **Leaning toward hiding:** Wes (pro).
- **Keep them, but stop calling it a gamble:** Jo (pro) and Jess K (rec). For them it's sequencing, and reading the hooks inside each color is the real game.
- **Keep them:** everyone else, especially the kids, parents and new fans, who rely on white as their safe ground.
- **What it means:** the order only looks solved if the colors are right *for you*. Jess K found a red Arsenal tile easier than a white Liga MX one. Jo found a red "Thorns" safer than a white "mid." For a mixed audience, hooks already matter. My judgment: keep the colors at launch, and test hidden colors with a few serious fans during the playtest.

**How easy is easy?**
- **Whites too hard:** kids, parents and new fans (Ivy, Owen, Tessa, Theo, Dana, Devin, Jess M, Walt). Ivy said white means "easy for Dad."
- **Whites a bit of a tax:** college and pros (Mateo, Ren, Jo), though Wes said the whites take him 30 seconds and that's fine.
- **What it means:** skew white toward true basics. The experts lose half a minute. The beginners stay.

**Is hard too easy or too obscure?**
- **Too easy:** Jonah (high school), Ren and Mateo (college) worry about getting a hat trick most days.
- **Too obscure:** most others worry about coin flips on obscure trivia.
- **What it means:** both can be fixed the same way. Deep but gettable, with three believable choices on every card (Jonah, Ren, Mateo, Wes, Danny, Devin).

**History or current?**
- **History:** the lifelong fans (Mo, Walt) want history to carry weight.
- **Current:** kids and teens want today's stars (Owen, Theo).
- **Not old men's history in the reds:** Maddie (high school).
- **What it means:** mix eras on every board.

**Kid Slang**
- **Love it:** the kids (Ivy, Owen).
- **Roast it:** the teens (Tessa, Theo, Maddie, Jonah). "Bussin" got screenshotted as cringe by four of them.
- **A bridge to their kids:** parents and lifelong fans (Rob, Dana, Jess K, Mo, Walt, Danny).
- **Angry when it ends a strong run:** pros and college players.
- **What it means:** at most one a day (Maddie, Danny), never a red tile (Jo, Jess M), every card checked by real kids (Tessa, Theo), and a hook that makes it obvious it's slang (Walt).

**The share text**
- **Short only:** the kids and Walt. Three lines, no rows.
- **Add the count:** 8 of 18 said Nil after 5 right, or a Brace with 17, flattens what they did. Mo: "⚽⚽ Brace · 17."
- **Add a path row:** 13 of 18 want one row of tile colors in tap order, ending on the miss, like ⬜⬜🟨🟥❌. That's teens, college, pros, rec, parents and Jess M. Devin and Mo want only the color they went out on.
- **What it means:** a path row shows both bravery and the count. It just means nothing to a 10-year-old or her grandma.

**Streaks**
- **Want a quiet days-played number:** the teens (Tessa wants it most), plus several others, about 11.
- **No streaks at all:** parents, pros, rec and Walt (Ivy, Dana, Jo, Wes, Jess K, Walt).
- **What it means:** if you ever add one, it's a count that never resets and never nags.

**Dark mode**
- **Want it:** teens, college and new fans who play in bed.
- **Not before launch:** parents, pros and lifelong fans. Mo: "Spend that time on font size."

## 4. The roadmap

**Before launch**

1. **Build the daily plainly, with the cards you have.** A seeded 6/6/6 draw, a puzzle number, 18 tiles with hook and card color, tap any tile, means line after every answer, 6 chips a goal, one miss ends it. Show the center ball with 6 empty chip slots from the start, so the goal rule teaches itself (Jess M, Dana).
   - Why: it's the whole game, and Jonah and Mateo say prove it's fun ugly before making it pretty.
   - Serves: all 18.
2. **Save and lock the day.** Resume mid-run after a lock, a tunnel or an app switch. A finished day reopens to your result and when the next board drops. No replays.
   - Why: losing one run to a tunnel is the day a commuter quits. Replays make results meaningless.
   - Serves: parents, rec, high school, lifelong fans.
3. **End screen and share.** The missed card's answer and means line, the result word, how many you got right, and one-tap copy. Never show the hook or answer of the card that ended your day (Mateo, Mo).
   - Why: the miss is when people decide to come back, and the group text is the only growth plan.
   - Serves: all 18.
4. **Readability and speed pass.** Test at 320, 375 and 402 wide and on an iPad. Test with someone over 55, a slow 10-year-old reader and a moving car. Use big tap targets far apart. Flicker the marquee once, keep animations short and keep sound off.
   - Why: if a hook can't be read, the tile never gets tapped. If a button gets fat-fingered, the day ends unfairly.
   - Serves: kids, parents, lifelong fans, commuters.
   - My judgment: this test decides honeycomb vs a simpler board.
5. **Content pass (no code).**
   - Make every white kid-easy, and add a basics batch as white cards.
   - Make hard deep, not obscure.
   - Give every card three believable choices.
   - Recheck every current fact.
   - Add draw guardrails: still random, but no more than 2 or 3 from one league, at least one women's card and one rules card each day, and Kid Slang at most once and never red.
   - Why: content trust matters more than any feature, and bad random days tell people "not for you."
   - Serves: everyone, especially pros, women's-game fans and beginners.
6. **One week with the G14 team chat and parents, then triviela.net.** Add a few older fans and real players. Fix the difficulty tags by hand as they play (Jo), and try hidden colors with a few serious fans. Then point the domain, check the iMessage link preview and launch to a few team chats. Then stop building for two weeks (Mateo).
   - Why: real chats show who posts without being asked.
   - Serves: all 18.

**After launch**

7. **Reveal the untouched tiles once the day ends.** Answers and means lines, unscored.
   - Why: it's cheap, it's where the learning lands and it's what people argue about.
   - Serves: 10 of 18, mostly adults, pros and new fans. Theo warns it can spoil the board for friends who haven't played yet.
8. **The anonymous difficulty counter.**
   - Why: it replaces your hand retagging with real results.
   - Serves: all 18.
9. **Offside picture cards, clear-cut only.** Prototype three, each matched to an IFAB example, with no "Not involved" judgment calls at first (Jo, Wes).
   - Why: it's the most-wanted feature on the panel and the thing no other soccer game has.
   - Serves: all 18, especially beginners, new fans and players.

## 5. The cut list

- **Accounts, leaderboards, friends lists:** all 18.
- **Hints, jokers, skips, extra lives, ad-to-continue:** nearly all. Maddie: "The whole thing works because there's no bailout."
- **Endless, practice, archive or replaying the day:** nearly all.
- **Timers:** 14 of 18, led by the kids and commuters.
- **The 6 × 6 share grid:** everyone.
- **Push notifications, streak guilt, streak-freeze:** all who mentioned it, loudest from parents, pros and rec.
- **Bonus points or multipliers for reds:** Ren, Mateo, Jo, Wes.
- **More non-soccer categories:** 13 of 18. Mateo: "Kid Slang is the ceiling."
- **Category pickers, filters, or league labels on tiles:** Rob, Maddie, Ren, Danny, Jess M, Devin.
- **Tutorials, congrats text, confetti, badges, mascots:** most of the panel.
- **Long or unskippable animations, sound by default:** about 9 of 18.
- **Dark mode before launch:** Rob, Jonah, Jo, Jess K, Mo, Walt.
- **The final boss or any meta-game on top of the daily:** Jonah.
- **Anti-cheat:** Jonah. **Guess-the-player puzzles:** Wes. **An app store app:** Maddie.

## 6. Open decisions for you

1. **Colors on tiles: shown, or hidden until answered?** My lean is to keep them shown and test hidden in the playtest.
2. **The share text.** Base only, base plus count ("Brace · 17"), or base plus one path row. My lean is the path row, since it carries the count and the bravery, with a dot or emoji legend nobody needs explained.
3. **Honeycomb or something simpler?** Let the readability test decide.
4. **Reset time.** Local midnight, like Wordle (Jonah, Jess K), or one global time (Ren, Mateo, Rob). The puzzle number keeps people comparing the same board either way.
5. **Kid Slang.** Keep it capped and kid-checked, or hold it out of the daily until real kids have vetted it.
6. **Card repeats and content pace.** My own math, not the panel's: the daily uses 18 cards a day, about 540 a month. 1,400 cards last about 77 days without repeats, and Owen noted that's 6 new whites every day. Do you allow cards to come back, and after how long? Jess M and Ren both said launch with what's approved, even if it's fewer categories.
7. **The full reveal after the day ends.** Most want it. Theo fears spoilers in the chat.
8. **Basics: its own named category or just white cards?** Most said just white cards.
9. **A quiet days-played number someday?** Teens yes, parents and pros no.

## 7. Three quotes

- "I learned white doesn't mean easy for ME. It means easy for Dad." (Ivy, 10, U10 rec defender)
- "There's no gamble here, it's sequencing." (Jo, 29, retired NWSL center back)
- "Seventeen right and my text says the same 'Brace' as twelve. That stung more than the miss." (Mo, 58, Sounders season ticket holder)

Where I added my own judgment, it's marked: the lean on colors, the honeycomb depending on the readability test, the path row recommendation and the card-repeat math. Everything else is the panel's.
